My read: the four sit on a spectrum from most map real-estate (edge dock) → most familiar (accordion) → most on-brand (map metaphor) → most information-dense (power-user). The strongest single idea across all four — "live state survives the collapse" — is compatible with any skeleton.

Rationale

Why edge-dock for AOP Drivers and trail buddies read this map mid-stage with one hand on a transmitter. They need terrain — contour, gates, mandatory-skill boundaries — to fill the screen at zoom 18+, not to peek out from behind a 360px panel. Edge-dock buys back the full canvas without forcing users to remember a keyboard shortcut; the icon rail keeps re-expand to one click.

Risk The icon rail competes with MapLibre's bottom-left attribution and (if added later) a scalebar. Pin the rail to top-left only, leave the bottom 80px of viewport-left clear, and the rail can co-exist.

Delight detail When a stage is in happening state and the hot card is collapsed, the flame handle's red dot pulses on the same 2-second cadence as the calendar row badge — the rail mirrors the live state without the user opening it. Same heartbeat, two surfaces.

Rationale

Why accordion beats edge-dock Drivers and stage buddies use this rail in two modes: planning ("what's happening Sat morning?") and orienting on the map ("where is that?"). Both modes return to the same three cards in the same order — the eye learns the stack. Edge-dock breaks that learned position. Accordion keeps pill → search → hot → calendar locked vertically so muscle memory survives the collapse, and composes with the existing .calendar-card.collapsed / .panel-section.collapsed patterns.

Real-estate risk Three collapsed strips at ~36px each + gaps + 44px pill bar = ~150px on desktop, ~135px on mobile. That's ~21% of a 720-tall viewport. Mitigation: collapsed strips compose into a single 28px row only when all three are idle (no active query, no hot event, no live session) — saves another ~70px when the park is quiet.

Delight detail Single-open behavior + state-badge persistence: tap one strip open and the others auto-collapse, but every strip keeps its live ● dot so a Rock Crawl about to start never disappears behind a search expansion.

Reading it

  • The ╔══╗ outer rule is the binder ring spine of a three-ring field binder hanging on the wall. The three cards inside are loose-leaf pages clipped onto the rings. One master toggle at the top — [ ⟨⟨ ] "fold spine closed" — collapses every page at once.
  • Search → ◢ paper-clip-and-folded-corner. The dog-ear is the affordance.
  • Hot Now → ◢ same dog-ear, but the Event lane carries a thin rust ribbon that hangs out the right side when a stage is live.
  • Calendar → ◢ dog-ear, plus the three tabs render as field-tag spines sticking up out of the top of the card. The active tab's spine sits flush; inactive ones recede.
  • Calendar days are rendered with a horizontal tear between dates — perforation, like a tear-off pad.

Rationale

Metaphor in one line The left rail is a three-page field binder hanging on the wall of the AOP registration trailer — search slip on top, today's hot-stage card in the middle, the weekend's schedule folder on the bottom.

Why it reinforces the source-traceable promise Paper artifacts carry attribution by default. A taped corner says "someone clipped this here." A ribbon says "this is live right now." A tab spine says "there's more behind this." The map's whole northstar — every line carrying where it came from, how much we trust it, whether it's publishable — already wants to look like a working binder, not a SaaS dashboard.

Risk Twee. If the dog-ear gets drawn with a literal shadow gradient and a paper-fiber texture it slides into Etsy-card territory. Hold it to the existing cream/brown/rust/moss palette and one-pixel borders. No drop shadows beyond what's already in the CSS.

Delight detail When a stage flips to LIVE, the Event-lane rust ribbon on the collapsed binder extends by ~12px with a slow ease — a paper streamer being clipped on by an invisible hand. No sound. No bounce. Just a quiet "something new is pinned up."

Rationale

Why power-user defaults fit this map Event staff and the map curator live on this viewer for hours during a busy weekend, plus dense iteration during code-health passes. Persona work names Host/AOP Staff as a primary user with provenance needs and a Driver who navigates by next-session anchors. Both benefit from terse, state-rich chrome (zoom readout, source-mode chip, rig-count freshness) and keyboard jumps to "now" / "next" — the things a radio-holding marshal asks for.

Risk First-time visitors and families will not discover h, t, n, or the strip glyphs. Mitigation: every kbd hint is mirrored in tooltips, the ? HUD is one keystroke away, and the mobile bottom-sheet pattern is touch-native — no shortcut required to operate.

Subtle delight The focus-visible outline (today a uniform moss alpha) becomes a preset-aware ring: moss in Park, slate in Topo, rust in Trace. The rail tells you which map you're driving without a label.
