# Show & Shine Northstar

TL;DR:
- A show & shine is the builder stage inside an RC trail event.
- At AOP, it should reward story, scale realism, craftsmanship, and trail-credible accessories. Not just polish.
- For the first Trail Blazing event, the theme should be an assignment: build a rig for the first crew sent into the Cove.

#show-and-shine #trail-blazing #events #scale-crawling

-----

This doc defines what "show & shine" means for Trail Blazing and future AOP RC events.

The map northstar still wins. The show & shine is event culture, not map truth. But it should rhyme with the map: scouts, wayfinding, field notes, route marking, recovery, camp, night work, and the first useful evidence coming back from the terrain.


## What a show & shine is

A show & shine is the parked-builder moment of a trail weekend.

Drivers line up rigs where people can inspect them. Judges, sponsors, or the crowd pick winners. The point is not only the prettiest truck. The point is the build: the body, weathering, stance, interior, wiring, lights, accessories, story, and whether the whole thing feels like a believable scale vehicle.

In full-size car culture, "shine" often means clean paint. In scale crawling, that is too narrow. A rusty trail truck, a dusty overland support rig, a beat-up recovery truck, and a polished hardbody can all belong. The deciding question is:

> What job does this rig look ready to do?

That question matters because scale RC is a building hobby. The show & shine gives people a reason to plan a rig before the event, buy or make accessories, and show the work without turning the weekend into a formal model contest.


## Research read

The pattern across sister events and published RC material is consistent.

AxialFest uses "Concours" / show & shine as a standing event surface, with categories such as Daily Driver, Truck, Military, Monster Truck, Buggy, Diva, Trailer, Most Adventurist, Best Theme, Best Cage, Best Paint, Scale Excellence, Rust Bucket, and Kids. One AxialFest format had category winners narrowed to top-three groups, then peers voted Best of Show.

Pro-Line By The Fire treats the show as part of the broader adventure weekend, not a detached contest. Its published schedules put Concourse / Show N' Shine beside G6 trails, GPS treasure, night crawls, Proving Grounds, Scale City, awards, vendors, and camp life. Recent public pages also show whole-event themes like Rockabilly, Old West, and Survival.

FMS FMStival's published show & shine categories make the same point in plain language: Adventurer, Best Themed Rig, Most Original, Overlander, Beauty Queen, Rust Bucket, Most Scale Accessories, and Best Overall. That is the useful spread: story, originality, trail role, condition, accessories, and top build.

WE RC ROCK's concours rules put the center of gravity where it belongs: scale realism is the most important criterion, and categories include Best Theme / livery, Best Interior, Shelf Queen, Most Detailed, Most Original, and Best in Show.

RCExcitement's RC4WD Go Scale Day local event copy frames scale day as not-a-competition, then tells drivers to bring winches, tow ropes, sand ladders, and their scale rigs. That is the right tone: social, practical, accessory-friendly.

Recon G6 and Axial material are useful as vocabulary, even when they are not show-specific. G6-style events require scale accessories and emphasize tow straps, recovery items, tools, and practical trail equipment. SORRCA scale rules also give a mature accessory vocabulary: winches, sand ladders, tow straps, interior, driver figures, scale engines, trailers, fuel cells, tools, shovels, jacks, fire extinguishers, and other small details.

The takeaway: a good show & shine theme is a build prompt. It should make a driver think, "What does my rig carry, and why?"


## Trail Blazing theme posture

Trail Blazing is the first event. Don't make the show theme a literal "trail blazing" sticker pack.

Recommended theme:

## The Advance Party

Prompt:

Your rig is part of the first crew sent ahead into Ellis Cove. It might survey, mark, recover, document, light, camp, or support the route. The best builds look like they have a job and came back with evidence.

Why this works:

- It is close to "scout build" without sounding like a kids' troop theme.
- It matches the first-event story: before the event has history, someone has to go first.
- It gives builders accessory lanes without requiring everyone to build the same truck.
- It fits the map project: the first useful work is finding, marking, and reporting what is true.
- It lets clean, rusty, military, overland, work truck, trail beater, trailer, and night-run builds all compete under one idea.

Other names in the same family:

- Field Notes
- Wayfinders
- Outriders
- Survey Run
- Basecamp Crew
- Recovery Detail
- Night Shift
- The Marking Crew
- First Out

Use those as category names or future themes. Don't use all of them at once.


## Future event themes (proposed)

The Advance Party is the first-event theme. These are seeds for the events after
it. Same test as always -- "what does this rig carry, and why?" -- so each is a
build prompt, not a livery pack. Pick one per event. Most of these crack open a
name from the family list above into a full theme; the last two are whole-event
themes in the Pro-Line "Rockabilly / Old West / Survival" mold.

### Recovery Detail

Prompt: your rig's job is getting other rigs unstuck. Winch, straps, snatch
blocks, traction boards, a jib or crane, a spare-everything box.

Why it fits: AOP doesn't penalize winching or recovery -- the format already
treats recovery as honest trail work, so a theme built on it rhymes with the
event instead of fighting it. Run it on a stage day where rigs actually get
stuck and the recovery builds have something to do.

Welcomes: wreckers, dedicated tow rigs, 6x6 support trucks, the buddy's rig.
Award angle: Recovery Lead. Good sponsor fit (a winch or strap maker).

### Night Shift

Prompt: your rig works after dark. Rock lights, light bar, work lamps, beacon,
reflective tape, a believable power source.

Why it fits: maps straight onto a Pro-Line "Into The Night" dusk run. Judge it
at dusk so the lights are the point, not a spec sheet.

Welcomes: anything kitted for low light -- survey, recovery, or camp rigs all
qualify if they're built to run dark. Award angle: Night Scout.

### Basecamp Crew

Prompt: your rig is the weekend. It carries camp -- roof tent, kitchen, water,
fuel, fridge, chairs, the awning.

Why it fits: this is Pro-Line By The Fire camp life at 1/10. Overland is the
single biggest scale-build lane and this theme is built straight for it.

Welcomes: overlanders, expedition rigs, support trailers. Award angle: Camp
Support. Pairs naturally with an actual on-site camp.

### The Marking Crew

Prompt: your rig lays out the course. Flags, stakes, gate markers, survey tape,
clipboards, GPS, paper maps, antennas.

Why it fits: the theme that rhymes hardest with the map project -- the first
useful work is finding, marking, and reporting what's true. Tighter and more
wayfinding-focused than The Advance Party, so it works as its own later event or
as a category inside one.

Welcomes: survey trucks, trail-crew rigs, anything with a wayfinding loadout.
Award angle: Best Field Detail.

### Old World Expedition

Prompt: your rig is a sponsored expedition truck from the golden age of
overlanding. Camel Trophy sand-yellow or G4 livery, roof rack, sand ladders, the
full period kit.

Why it fits: this is the cultural root G6 itself names -- Camel Trophy and the
Land Rover G4 Challenge. A whole-event theme with a strong look that still
demands real accessories, not just a paint job.

Welcomes: Defenders, Land Cruisers, period expedition builds, anyone chasing a
livery. Award angle: Best Theme / livery.

### The Convoy

Prompt: your rig is one truck in a relief convoy after the storm -- comms,
medical, water, fuel, recovery, or lighting. No single hero truck; the build has
to make sense as part of a column.

Why it fits: a whole-event theme (Survival-adjacent) that opens every accessory
lane at once and rewards rigs that look like they belong to a bigger operation.
Good for a club turnout or a high-headcount event.

Welcomes: comms trucks, field ambulances, water/fuel haulers, recovery, command
rigs. Award angle: Best of Show goes to the truck you'd most want in the column.


## Accessory lanes

The theme should invite builders to add details with a job.

Wayfinding:
- paper maps, topo sheets, clipboards, pencils, route cards
- trail flags, stakes, cones, gate markers, survey tape
- compass, GPS unit, radios, antennas

Recovery:
- winch, tow straps, D-rings, pulleys, snatch blocks
- sand ladders, traction boards, jack, shovel, spare tire
- tool roll, toolbox, spare parts box

Field support:
- water cans, fuel cans, cooler, first-aid kit, fire extinguisher
- tarp, bedroll, small tent, camp chair, cook kit
- trailer, utility rack, bed rack, roof rack

Night work:
- rock lights, headlamps, light bars, work lamps
- beacon, reflective tape, glow markers

Documentation:
- camera, field notebook, route tags, sample box
- "checked" tags, inspection cards, small signboard

Weathering:
- trail dust, mud, scrapes, patched panels, used recovery gear
- believable wear beats random damage

Accessory rule of thumb:

If the accessory doesn't tell the rig's job, it is roof-rack confetti.


## Judging posture

Keep judging simple. This is a first event, not a national concours.

Judges should ask:

- What is this rig's job?
- Does the whole build tell the same story?
- Is it scale believable next to AOP terrain?
- Are the accessories attached, sized, and purposeful?
- Does the craftsmanship hold up close?
- Would this rig make sense at registration, the trailhead, the night run, or the recovery point?

Optional score shape:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Theme / story | 25 |
| Scale realism | 25 |
| Craftsmanship / finish | 20 |
| Trail readiness | 15 |
| Field details / accessories | 10 |
| Presentation | 5 |

Do not use SORRCA scale points as the show score. They are useful vocabulary, not the point of this event.


## First-event award set

Use fewer awards than the internet makes tempting.

Recommended first pass:

- Best of Show -- strongest complete build.
- The Advance Party -- best theme execution.
- Best Field Detail -- best small-scale story details.
- Recovery Lead -- best recovery / trail-service setup.
- Camp Support -- best overland, trailer, or support loadout.
- Night Scout -- best lighting / after-dark build.
- Trail Worn -- best believable weathering.
- People's Choice -- crowd vote.

Add a youth award if there are enough youth entries to make it real. Don't make a lonely kid category.

Avoid "cleanest" as a main award. AOP's scale identity is rocks, mud, water, grades, and trail work. Clean paint can win Best of Show if the build earns it, but clean is not the northstar.


## Event operations

Home base: Pavilion.

Schedule shape:
- line up after driver meeting or before awards
- require entries to be staged by a clear cutoff
- judge while rigs are parked and owners can answer questions
- announce winners at awards / raffle

Collect at entry:
- driver name
- rig name
- scale / tire class if useful
- build story in one sentence
- chosen category, if categories require pre-entry
- permission to photograph for event recap

Minimum display needs:
- table or marked lineup lane
- number cards
- basic lighting if judged near dusk
- one photo station or consistent backdrop
- People's Choice ballot / QR if voting is used

Sponsor fit:
- a vendor can sponsor one category, especially Recovery Lead, Night Scout, Field Detail, or People's Choice
- the sponsor can help judge that category
- don't let a sponsor turn the whole theme into a product catalog


## Copy block

Short public version:

> Show & Shine Theme: The Advance Party. Build a rig for the first crew sent into the Cove. Survey truck, trail marker, recovery runner, camp support, night scout -- bring the details that tell the job. Clean, rusty, muddy, polished, handmade, or hardbody all work. The best builds look scale, trail-credible, and ready to report back.

Shorter:

> Build the rig that goes first.


## What not to do

- Don't make "Trail Blazing" mean flame decals and trail signs only.
- Don't reward accessory piles with no story.
- Don't make a scale-point tech inspection unless the event explicitly needs one.
- Don't bury the beginner. A smart $20 detail can beat an expensive pile of parts if the story is better.
- Don't make show & shine compete with trail time. It should frame the day, not swallow it.
- Don't store individual vehicle profiles in the AOP map database. Winners and photos can be event recap content later; the map doesn't need a garage.
