# What's This For

TL;DR:
- AOP is a real-world park where people drive radio-controlled scale trail trucks.
- The map serves drivers, trail buddies, event organizers, and visitors -- not 4x4 owners and not generic hikers.
- The center of gravity is Recon G6-style scale rally events, Pro-Line By The Fire-style multi-day adventures, and the broader scale crawling hobby.
- If you treat AOP like an off-highway-vehicle park, you will draw the wrong map.

This doc exists so a new session, contributor, or AI agent doesn't have to reconstruct the world AOP lives in. The northstar doc says what the map promises. This one says what the map is *for*.

If anything below contradicts the northstar promise, the northstar wins. But the northstar is short by design, and a short northstar plus the wrong mental model produces a confidently wrong map.


## The world the map serves

AOP -- Adventure Off Road Park, South Pittsburg, Tennessee -- is private land set up for radio-controlled scale trail trucking and rock crawling. Roughly 600 acres on record, currently mapped as a working envelope from two Marion County parcels.

The drivers are people, not vehicles. The "trucks" are 1/10 scale electric scale crawlers and trail trucks -- Axial SCX10s, Traxxas TRX-4s, RC4WD builds, kit-built rigs with portal axles, two-speed transmissions, locking diffs, scale accessories (jerry cans, bed rolls, winches, rock lights), and aftermarket bodies that mimic real 4x4s.

The trails are real terrain at real-terrain scale -- AOP's rocks, ledges, gullies, water crossings, mud pits, and grades exist at 1:1. What changes is the vehicle. A 12" gate at AOP is a meaningful obstacle for a 1.9"-tire SCX10. A 100-foot stage is a real run, not a stroll. The map's job is to draw the terrain accurately so a 1/10-scale rig and its driver can read it from board, kiosk, phone, or print.

This is not an off-highway-vehicle park. There are no Jeeps on trail, no UTVs, no dirt bikes. Trail width, slope rules, and "is this driveable" judgments are all scale-crawler judgments, not 1:1 OHV judgments. Get this part wrong and every downstream call about layer styling, course widths, and difficulty ratings goes sideways.


## Who the map is for

**Drivers.** Hobbyists driving scale crawlers. They want to know where the trails are, what the gates look like, what's seasonally open, where staging is, and which sections their rig can handle. Reading a 600-acre property by walking it is impractical; the map is how they orient.

**Trail buddies.** In G6-style events, every driver is paired with a stage buddy (a stage marshal who walks the stage, marks time penalties, and runs the penalty winch if the driver's rig doesn't have one). The buddy reads the map alongside the driver. The print board is largely for them.

**Event organizers.** AOP staff and visiting series (Recon G6, Pro-Line, regional clubs) need to lay out stages, gates, mandatory skills sections, day loops, geocache waypoints, and staging zones on top of the same trails. Course layout is a primary map workflow, not an afterthought.

**Visitors and prospectives.** People considering a trip, scouting the park before showing up, or following an event remotely. The public web map is mostly for them, with a "you are here" function for people physically on-site.

**AOP itself.** As a landowner, AOP needs the map to show the property line, internal zones (open / closed / staging / vendor / camping), and how published trail data attributes back to AOP's permission. The source register and `permission` field exist for AOP, not for the drivers.

If a feature on the map doesn't serve at least one of these audiences, it doesn't belong on the map.


## The hobby in one read

Scale RC crawling is a building hobby first, a driving hobby second. The culture rewards a rig that looks right and survives -- not necessarily one that wins. Two overlapping subcultures:

**Comp crawlers** are competition-tuned. Lighter chassis, optimized geometry, often less scale-accurate. Classes break out as Street / Weekend Warrior / Outlaw, with explicit gate widths (typically 11" or 12" minimum) and penalty scoring -- rolls, reverses, missed gates, gate touches. Lowest score wins, like golf. Courses are short, technical, judged.

**Scale trail trucks** (sometimes "scalers" or "trail rigs") are built to look and feel like a 1:1 rig. SCX10 and TRX-4 chassis with realistic bodies, working winches, scale accessories, often slower and heavier than comp rigs. The point is the journey, the photo, the build authenticity. They dominate G6, Pro-Line By The Fire, and most multi-day scale-adventure events.

AOP sits more on the scale-trail side, with comp-course capability layered on. The map needs to support both vocabularies cleanly.

**Vehicle vocabulary** that should not surprise the map's author:
- 1/10 scale -- the dominant size class.
- 1.9" / 2.2" / 1.55" -- wheel/tire sizes. 1.9" is the comp standard and most-popular all-around; 2.2" is the larger-rig look (Wraith, RR10); 1.55" is the smaller "tiny trucks" niche.
- Portal axles, two-speed transmission, locking front/rear differentials -- common drivetrain features.
- Winch, dig, rear steer -- features that determine event eligibility. G6 explicitly bans dig and rear steer.
- Scale accessories -- jerry cans, bed rolls, coolers, shovels, rock lights, winches. G6 requires at least 5.
- Trail buddy, stage marshal, penalty winch -- event roles, not gear.
- RTR vs kit -- "Ready-To-Run" (assembled) vs build-it-yourself. Both are common.


## The G6 family of events

Recon G6 is the dominant scale-adventure format in the hobby, and the one most likely to land at AOP. Founded by Brian Parker. First event in late 2010. The series sits on top of an Axial SCX10 license (Axial has been the "Official Truck of the RECON G6" for years). The cultural inspirations Parker has talked about are the Camel Trophy and the Land Rover G4 Challenge -- adventure-flavored team trials, not stadium racing.

The G6 spirit Parker named: **us vs the terrain**, not me vs you. Family-friendly, all ages, all skill levels, fun first, competition second. The map should respect that. If a layer styling makes AOP look like a stadium track, it's misreading the audience.

### Recon G6 Challenge -- the rally format

The base G6 event:

- **3 trails per event**, each marked with 50 to 200 course markers.
- **2-4 stages** per trail, depending on the event.
- Drivers run each stage **against the clock**, navigating numbered gates in order, between the gates (cross a boundary, take a penalty).
- **No penalty** for winching, using a tow strap, or reversing. Penalty for hitting a gate, hitting a boundary, or being kicked / caught / stopped by the driver. The rule is "you may not assist your own vehicle."
- **Mandatory skills sections** sit inside each stage. They have an entrance and an exit ribbon, and must be driven. Examples Parker has used: driving through mud / water / snow / sand, a steep up-grade or down-grade, a tarmac segment, a taco stand (a tight obstacle named for the shape, not the food), a pull-sled full pull.
- **Stage buddy** -- not a co-driver. Walks the stage, marks penalties and bonuses, runs the penalty winch if the driver doesn't have one. After the driver finishes the stage, they swap.
- **Vehicle rules:** 1/10 electric, scale chassis (MSD, RC4WD, Axial SCX-10), 1.9" or 1.55" wheels and tires only, minimum 5 scale accessories, no rear steer, no dig, 4x4 or 2x4 fine.
- **Spirit rule:** maximum driving time, fun first, "us vs the terrain."

### G6 Enduro / The FIX Enduro -- the endurance format

The endurance variant. Less common than the base G6 but the format that matters when the user says "enduro G6":

- **10-12 hour run** on an 18-lap-class technical course.
- **Solo driver** or a **team taking turns on a single vehicle** -- the vehicle is what's enduring, not necessarily one human.
- Course conditions deliberately punishing: water, rocks, mud, steep grades, extreme weather. "Premier scale endurance event on the planet" is the marketing, but the format is genuinely brutal on rigs.
- Same 1.55 / 1.9 / 2.2 wheel-and-tire eligibility as base G6, with stock-from-manufacturer requirement.
- The relevant cultural artifact is **Recon G6 Certified** -- a durability badge given to aftermarket parts that survive the format. The badge is hobbyist-facing reputation, not a sticker; it signals "this part won't disintegrate in 10 hours of abuse."

What the AOP map has to support for a G6 Enduro:
- A single closed-loop course of meaningful length.
- Lap counting infrastructure (lap-line position, timing point).
- Multiple terrain types along the same loop -- water, rock, mud, grade segments must be drawable as distinct feature attributes.
- Pit / driver-change zone.
- A way to publish lap progress to spectators (out of scope for V1, but the data spine should not make it harder).

### Other named G6 variants and offshoots

- **Asian Scale Invasion** / Return of the Dragon -- the regional G6 license in Asia.
- **AxialFest Recon G6** -- the flagship G6 historically run alongside AxialFest.
- **Belgium Recon G6** and other regional events -- the format travels.
- **Malvado G6 / Rock Riot** -- early regional G6-adjacent events documented on crawler forums.

The point of listing these is not that AOP needs to host all of them, but that "G6" is a family of events with shared DNA -- numbered gates, mandatory skills sections, stage buddies, scale-accessory rules, fun-first ethos. The AOP map should support the shape, not lock to a specific event's rulebook.


## Other event formats AOP is likely to host

### Pro-Line By The Fire -- the multi-day adventure

Held annually in Apple Valley, CA, run by Pro-Line Racing. One of the premier scale events in the country. The format is what AOP-as-festival-venue most resembles:

- **Cloverleaf day loops** -- multiple day routes radiating from a central staging area.
- **GeoCache treasure hunt** -- placed waypoints with hints, drivers find them during the day.
- **Photo Scavenge** -- waypoint list of photos to take in specific locations.
- **"Into The Night" dusk run** -- a route designed for rock lights and headlamps.
- **Pro-Line Proving Grounds** -- a competitive course.
- **Scale Trials** -- structured trial format.
- **Interco Mega Mud Truck Challenge** -- specialty competition.
- **Sumo-Wars** -- vehicle-vs-vehicle pushing contest.
- **Camping on-site** (first-come), food vendor area, registration / wristband check.

The AOP map needs to render this whole footprint on top of trails: loops, waypoints, course markers, staging, vendor, camping, parking. Same data spine, layered on by event.

### Comp-class gate courses

Shorter, more competitive runs. Class rules from clubs like WE RC ROCK, CanyonRC, and 997 RC Raceway govern gate widths (11" / 12" minimums), penalty scoring, class definitions (Street / Weekend Warrior / Outlaw or local equivalents), and course count (typically 4-6 per class). The AOP map should be able to render these as event-bound layers without disrupting the trail base.

### Scale Trials

Slow-driving accuracy events. Gates and obstacles laid out as a precision course. Less "rally" and more "trials" in the European motorcycle-trials sense. Often run alongside G6 or as standalone club events. Same map shape as comp courses, different vocabulary on the legend.


## What this means for the map

Every design decision should pass through "does this serve a scale RC trail driver, a stage buddy, an event organizer, or AOP itself?"

Specifically:

- **Difficulty / rating language** uses Fun / Access / Challenge (rcmap.io's axes are the established RC vocabulary). Don't import 4x4 OHV difficulty scales.
- **Gate width and class** are real attributes on gate features, not free text. Width is small enough to matter at 1/10 scale; class governs eligibility.
- **Stage** is a first-class feature: polygon (or corridor) with start, finish, boundary, and an ordered list of gate IDs.
- **Mandatory skills section** is a sub-feature inside a stage with explicit entrance and exit, plus a type tag (mud / water / sand / tarmac / grade / pull-sled / taco-stand / other).
- **Day loop, geocache waypoint, photo waypoint, dusk-run route** are real feature types for multi-day adventures.
- **Lap-based course** with lap-line and timing-point features supports Enduro-class events.
- **Stage buddy role** is a person concept, not a map feature -- but the print board should be readable while walking the stage, which is a real layout constraint.
- **Permission scope** (`public` / `internal` / equivalent) aligns to the Scaletra "Hide My Map" / public-friends-private mental model so AOP staff and visiting series share vocabulary.
- **Event status** (`announced` / `registration_open` / `live` / `closed` / `archived`) drives layer visibility on the public viewer.
- **Vehicle vocabulary** belongs in the legend, not in the data: "1.9" gate", "1.55-class course", "no-dig stage". Don't try to model vehicles in the map database; tag features by what vehicle rules they assume.


## What not to do

- Don't import OHV trail-difficulty schemes (Tread Lightly, BLM ratings, mountain-bike singletrack ratings). They're the wrong abstraction.
- Don't model individual drivers' vehicles in the AOP database. That's Scaletra's job.
- Don't build a registration / ticketing / scoring system. AOP can use event-specific tools; the map links out.
- Don't treat "events" as a separate map. Events ride on the same trail spine -- a gate on a stage is a feature in the same PostGIS database as a trail centerline.
- Don't write hobby vocabulary out of the map in the name of being beginner-friendly. A driver who shows up at AOP already speaks this language. Sanding it down to generic park language confuses them.
- Don't assume "off-road" means what it means in the OHV world. At AOP, it means scale RC. Read every backlog item and every northstar promise with that filter.
