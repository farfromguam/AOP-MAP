# narration modes

short reference for me, when I'm writing prose vignettes and need to remember what tool does what.

## the three modes

every line on the page is doing one of these jobs:

- **dialogue** — what's spoken aloud. quotes.
- **action / blocking** — what physically happens. who does what to whom.
- **narration** — the storyteller telling you about it.

two more that often slip in:

- **interiority** — a character's thoughts, internal state.
- **aside** — narrator briefly winking at the reader.

most confusion in a draft comes from one line trying to do two of these at once.

## what the symbols actually mean

- `( )` parens — *aside or modifier*. lower-priority info inside the main flow. in screenplay: a small note on *how* a line is delivered — `(quietly)`, `(under his breath)`.
- `[ ]` brackets — *intrusion or stage direction*. something outside the spoken text. in plays/transcripts: non-verbal action — `[door slams]`, `[laughter]`.

so the assignment is:

- emotional state / delivery modifier -> `( )`
- external action / what's happening on the stage -> `[ ]`

mixing the two — or letting one symbol carry two jobs — is what makes a hybrid feel "off" even when nothing is grammatically wrong.

## the secret weapon: free indirect discourse

narration that takes on a character's voice without quotes or brackets:

> she peeled another. *fruit?* it was a big bag. he kept eating.

"fruit?" is his thought. "it was a big bag" is his realization. no brackets, no italics needed — the narrator is *speaking through him*. most modern short prose runs on this. it's what makes sedaris feel intimate and lydia davis feel surgical. when in doubt, this is the move.

## format conventions — pick one and commit

- **screenplay** — CHARACTER cues in caps, `(parenthetical)` for delivery, plain action lines, sparse.
- **stage play** — name + colon for dialogue, italicized or `[bracketed]` stage directions.
- **prose** — flows together; punctuation, tense, and free indirect do the work. brackets rare.
- **screenplay-hybrid** (what oranges.md is) — bare dialogue in quotes, `[action]`, `(delivery)`, bare prose for narrator. works *if* the cue grammar is locked.

## glossary

- **POV** — first person ("I"), third limited ("he", inside one head), third omniscient ("he", narrator knows all).
- **tense register** — present ("he eats") = immediate. past ("he ate") = recounted. shifting mid-piece is a tool, not a mistake.
- **beat** — a small pause or moment of reaction.
- **dialogue tag** — "he said," "she whispered." often best omitted.
- **free indirect** — narrator borrowing a character's voice (above).
- **aside** — narrator addressing the reader directly.
- **vignette / flash fiction** — short, image-driven, single-scene piece. what most of this dir is.

## the audit question

when something feels off, ask line by line: *what mode is this, and does its punctuation match?* the slip is almost always a line where two modes are jammed together with the wrong symbol around them.
