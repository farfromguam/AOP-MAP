What problem for you does it solve?


I was asked this question and had to pause. we are close to showing this to people.
... and I had not really answered this for myself.
talking about the app was difficult largely because it had been in my head all the time.

It can do so many things... but why?

-----

The original vision was for my business. We converted vehicles from internal combustion to electric power. 

the idea was to have a workflow that could be duplicated and modified per project.
One comes in and it takes x attributes.
another comes in and a subset of that work is similar and a subset is different.
some is exactly the same...


most importantly the shop was spatially laid out in the world grid.
each car and workbench had items attached. Shelves had inventory...

it was a digital twin of the work process inventory and task assignment.

machines were born from the idea that we would start with a more or less blank slate and 
one machine would customize that output artifact and push to a queue.

each task would begin inside a dependency tree and that would carry forward.


We could have used something off the shelf. Every off-the-shelf product is a series of screens you navigate through. One task view, a stack of pages you click through. Never fun.


Both my business partner and I are neurodivergent. The overhead of feeding someone else's workflow is its own tax -- we were already building a shop, I wasn't going to also build a tolerance for someone else's app.

Second to that. I knew how software could integrate, we had already been managing our finances with a custom workflow.
I did not want to fully commit to one ecosystem yet. I figured... If we were making it work with finances maybe we could make something simple work for task management. 

What we needed was a kanban board. Visual. Cards. Moving. That's it. I'd been writing software for a  
decade and had shipped harder things, so the question wasn't whether we could build it. 
It was what we wanted it to do.

He showed me a 4x4 drawing about card prioritization where the top left is most important and bottom right is trash... I got to thinking about what was possible. (It was a derivation of the Eisenhower Matrix -- 2x2 grid, urgency on one axis, importance on the other. Top-left = Do, top-right = Schedule, bottom-left = Delegate, bottom-right = Delete.)

One tech spike in, the board wasn't a board. Cards lived where the work lived -- on a vehicle, a workbench, a shelf. The shop was the UI. I could print a task, walk to it, and think through the next day on my feet.

-----

now. what problem.

The business is no longer a thing. I had so much energy and emotion committed to this.
the app never fully got to be realised.

It's been gnawing at me. It pains me like a parrot on my shoulder desperately trying to maintain balance in the sea.

It haunts me.

I keep thinking of this world I built in my head the interactions that could happen.
And I can't seem to drop it.

is it the desire to see it through.
am I masking some deeper loss. -- Am I trying to keep the dream alive?

I think it's closer to the fact that I can SEE the potential.

-----

I find great satisfaction in building things that cannot be bought.
Solving a problem that is so unique a product does not exist.

A shelf. not interesting. You got ikea dawg.

A shelf in the corner of a room that is 100 years old and not plumb true or level that needs to hold a marble collection.
I am your man.

this project seems to fit a number of criteria that intrigue me

-----

I have this "actual" use case for myself. I want to see that through.
Then I have visions of when future people use this app and it pushes me to see it through.

I want to finish. 
Quitting does not seem to be an option.
